## 1. Primitives, institutions, and timing

Keep three countries with one nationally owned producer each; SU has two
members and an outsider, SW has three national standards, IS one common
standard. The fixed formal partition precedes firms' private additional
standard support at real fixed cost F. One standard bought once applies in
both member destinations. Segmented markets, destination-specific
recognition, no capacity coupling and no global production spillovers are
essential to this factor-two technology. Supporting the member standard
in member destinations does not reciprocally change member access in the
outsider destination. Governments compare continuation welfare under fixed
counterfactual institutions; no treaty renegotiation equilibrium is added.

Demand follows consumer optimization with individually given expected
network benefits; suppliers anticipate fulfilled networks when choosing
their actual market strategy. For a differentiated affine subclass,

`U0(q)=1'q-q'M0q/2`, `M0=(1-gamma)I+gamma*11'`,
`G(q,s)=v*C(s)*q`, `p=1-(M0-v*C(s))*q`, `CS=q'M0q/2`.

C is the exact native/support group indicator, including singleton-zero
network benefits. For `gamma<1`, U0 is strictly concave. Consumer allocation
at arbitrary prices is determined by the nonnegative-demand complementarity
system if `M0-vC` is positive definite. That last property must be checked
for each compatibility state; it does not follow from full-compatible
positive definiteness alone.

At `gamma=1`, do not claim strict concavity or smooth differentiated demand.
Use the exact R13 atomistic uniform-type foundation: each buyer maximizes
`theta+g_i-p_i`, active net prices clear at `1-Q`, buyers are indifferent
among supported sellers, quantities supplied select their allocations,
and `CS=Q^2/2`. This exactly reproduces R9, not just a limit. Firms'
quantity commitments precede network fulfillment; ignoring the network
response in firm deviations would change the original model.

## 2. Costs, welfare, and conduct

Native cost is zero, foreign unsupported cost c, additional-standard
support leaves residual d with `0<=d<=c`; support costs F per additional
standard. A full binary investment changes BOTH cost and network group;
varying d in the post-adoption state holds the new network fixed.

`W_i=CS_i+sum_dest pi_i^dest-F*(additional standards of firm i)`.
Only the single nationally owned firm's worldwide profit enters. The
identity `mC=CS+2*pi_member` uses two symmetric destinations; it is not
regional aggregate welfare or the addition of foreign-owned profits.

Cournot maximizes own quantity profit anticipating G. Bertrand maximizes
own price profit anticipating the fulfilled-demand allocation, including
rival exits/entry. Both concepts are pure product-market Nash; investment
Nash permits mixed actions. The new price proofs concern pure price Nash,
not uniqueness of mixed price equilibria.

In a fully compatible destination, the common own-markup coefficient ell
is DERIVED from the specified game, rather than a free primitive:

- Cournot: `ell=1-v`.
- Differentiated Bertrand: `ell=(1-gamma)*(1+2gamma-3v)/(1+gamma-2v)`.

The common algebra uses ell after these separate game derivations. It
cannot establish existence for an unspecified competition mode.

## 3. Primitive restrictions versus endogenous objects

| Primitive/structural | Derived/endogenous |

| U0 or buyer-type distribution; gamma | equilibrium quantities, prices, profits |
| v, C(s), singleton rule | effective demand Jacobian and its inverse |
| c,d,F, real support technology | adoption gains, alpha,beta,sigma |
| two member destinations and segmentation | b_L,r_U,e_L,e_U,H,J,D0,D1 |

The new rho,A,tau,kappa expressions are explicit functions of gamma,v and
separately derived ell; they give a real primitive sign result for the
post-adoption component. They do not derive the initial SU advantage or
all investment thresholds on a common primitive domain.
