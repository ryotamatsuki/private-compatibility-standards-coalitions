## Closest-literature cross-check and implications

1. **Gandal & Shy (2001, JIE)**: regional unions and differentiated price competition are established; private firm post-coalition adoption *feedback* into same government's ex-post ranking is the bounded difference, not “first standards union”. Highest-status source venue is excluded for insufficient generality, with explicit justification.
2. **Takarada et al. (2020, JPET)**: multilateral/regional harmonization welfare and coalition effect are prior; policy wedge novelty must be private support *after* formal policy.
3. **Buccella–Fanti–Gori (2026, EINT)**: one-way endogenously chosen compatibility, variable costs, Cournot/Bertrand and welfare are known. R19 T-R algebra alone is not safe independent novelty.
4. **Cheng–Huang (2025, J Econ)**: product compatibility, network effects, competitive outcome; source undercuts overly broad “new network incompatibility” claims.
5. **Maggi–Mrázová (2024 / 2026)**: regulatory diversification and fixed per-standard adoption; July 2026 theorem-level priority still unavailable. Specific public/private timeline distinction must be bounded, not falsely settled.
6. **Walz–Woeckener DP3816**: domestic installed-base asymmetry, regional standards body and third-market compatibility; available abstracts pose an **unresolved** near-neighbor priority risk.

## Desk-reject review, by editorial completeness (not just mathematical correctness)

- JICT: paper must explain *what policy analyst can measure or predict* from residual unit support cost, coalition scope and firm incentives, without misrepresenting an empirically calibrated effect. User of original model can diagnose conditions, not automatically implement an optimal public policy.
- RNE: the story must be legible in a genuine network industry, not an abstract multilateral treaty model masquerading as telecommunications; the constructed witness is mathematical not empirical.
- RIO: precise research question and clearly distinct theoretical contribution are essential, and recent linear Cournot comparator shows allowed model specificity but says nothing about this submission being accepted.
- JPET: government comparative welfare does not by itself give an independently new public economics theorem when nearest JPET prior is about regionalism/multilateralism.
- EINT/ITJ: **publisher manuscript-preparation rule** outweighs topical fit in current provenance, independently of likely desk rejection.
