\section{A conduct-neutral two-channel response bound}
The inherited quantity-choice Cournot case uses atomistic buyers with
\(p_i=1-Q+G_i(q,s)\), \(CS=Q^2/2\), and firms anticipating fulfilled
network expectations. In a differentiated price-choice case a distinct
buyer microfoundation satisfies \(p=\nabla U(q)+G(q,s)\),
\(CS=U(q)-q^\top\nabla U(q)\), under the usual buyer-optimality
and market-clearing restrictions. Neither buyer welfare definition may be
changed solely to force a government ranking.

For one of two symmetric formal union members, write
\(m_B(z)\) for member-destination consumer surplus plus the two member
firms' local profits with the outsider incompatible and at cost wedge
\(z\); \(m_C(z)\) for the same national component with the outsider
fully compatible; \(f_B(z)\) for the home firm's export profit in the
outsider destination, where the two member firms have wedge \(z\);
and \(f_C\) for its fully compatible, zero-wedge export profit.
All these outcomes are obtained from a re-solved, specified product
market game. Let \(H=m_C(0)-m_B(0)\), \(J=f_C-f_B(0)\),
\(b=m_B'\), \(r=m_C'\), \(e=-f_B'\).
Then the SU-minus-IS national gaps are
\begin{align}
D_0&=\int_0^c b(z)\,dz-H-J-\int_0^c e(z)\,dz,\\
D_1&=\int_0^d r(z)\,dz-J-\int_0^c e(z)\,dz.
\end{align}
The export-market term is unchanged by the outsider's investment.

\begin{proposition}[A conditional scope--erosion mechanism; R18-P]
Suppose the induced consumer-optimal fulfilled-expectations market
continuations exist and are regular and uniquely selected; or suppose
that the following bounds hold uniformly across all admissible selections.
Suppose outsider support yields at least \(\alpha>0\) operating-profit
gain per each of two member destinations, while all SU members' reverse
one-interface gains are at most \(\beta\) across rival profiles
and all SW one-interface incremental gains are at most \(\sigma\).
Assume \(\max\{0,\beta,\sigma\}<F<2\alpha\).
If on \(z\in[0,c]\), \(b(z)\ge b_L\) and
\(e_L\le e(z)\le e_U\), and on \(z\in[0,d]\),
\(r(z)\le r_U\), then the two further inequalities
\[
c(b_L-e_U)>H+J,\qquad d r_U<J+c e_L
\]
imply unique outsider-only private SU adoption, no SW adoption,
and \(D_0>0>D_1\). The result holds independent of
the chosen Cournot/Bertrand first-order equations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All-profile incremental dominance makes member and SW additional
interfaces strictly unprofitable. With members thereby inactive, one
outsider interface covers two markets and yields at least \(2\alpha-F>0\).
Integrating the displayed derivative envelopes gives
\(D_0\ge c(b_L-e_U)-H-J>0\)
and \(D_1\le dr_U-J-ce_L<0\).
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Scope.} This is a \emph{response-envelope} theorem, not
yet a complete demand-primitive theorem: the required signs and bounds
are not proved for arbitrary consumer utility or conduct. Its finite
investment selection is not independently novel, nor is national
incidence accounting. The original R9 payoff blocks, residual
\(R(d,v)\), boundary cutoffs and limited coalition-stability theorem
remain the sharper original-model results.

\(L_0\approx0.0011264>0\), \(U_1\approx-0.0020701<0\).

\(L_0\approx0.0013170>0\),

primitives. The full global Bertrand price-deviation/entry proof is
\emph{inherited} from R16; no general nonlinear \(C^3\) price-game
uniqueness is proved here. At \(v=0,c=1/10\), the original Cournot
pre-investment ranking is negative, \(-47/3200\):
reversal is not universal.
