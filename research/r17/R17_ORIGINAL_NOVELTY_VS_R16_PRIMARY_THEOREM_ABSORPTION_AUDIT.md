## 2. Historical novelty ledger must NOT be overwritten

### R0 / Original IJIO (September 2026)
- **Existing:** 3-country standardization institution; linear Cournot product-market blocks; endogenous privately chosen outside standard; initial regional advantage \(\mathscr E-\mathscr D\) and subsequent erosion; coalition stable-set application.
- **Original novelty posture:** private adoption can erode rent of a public formal agreement, conditionally shifting members' national institutional welfare ranking.
- **What was missing:** economically primitive, competition-mode-independent causal interpretation rather than configuration-specific profit arithmetic.

### R1–R2
- **Rejected as independently new:** simple one-way private compatibility, fixed-fee economies of scope, dominant-strategy binary investment thresholds. R2 \`G1-E/U\` is directly absorbed by generic game-theoretic Nash/dominance logic; \`G1-S\` is partially absorbed by fixed-cost economies of scope (Gorman 1985; Cho–McCardle 2009; van Wegberg 2004). **Do not promote R16 G1 to a newly invented general theorem** simply because both Cournot and Bertrand satisfy its inequality.

### R3–R7
- **Surviving narrow contribution:** within canonical Cournot, strictly positive residual-cost relief \(d>0\), uniquely selected one-way firm adaptation, and explicit *success/strengthen-only/weaken* government-ranking sign partition. The residual-rent function \(R(d,v)\) is structurally consequential and requires equilibrium derivation.
- **Pre-specified negative:** R5's \(\gamma=1/2\) differentiated Cournot/Bertrand specification has no initial regional ranking advantage even when asymmetric private investment exists. This is a genuine failure and **must remain** after R16 success at *different parameters*.
- **Coalition stability:** secondary, institution-specific. Preference reversal is NOT an actual endogenous post-investment treaty-switching equilibrium; its mapping to five-partition blocking is certified only for original stated circumstances.

### R9
- **Verdict at 2026-09-22:** “NO NEW DIRECT ABSORPTION FOUND; LIMITED NOVELTY stands,” targeted to JICT as conditional applied theory. The old report explicitly forbade blanket “competition-form independent” claims on the *then-available evidence*.
- **R16 updates the evidence**, but does not retroactively falsify R9's time-stamped audit: a model-neutral conditional theorem is now shown **nonempty in two distinct conduct modes**. It does not imply every model or every parameter vector reverses.

### R12–R16 new science (separate, later provenance)
- **G1:** stringent incentive dominance over all SU/SW adoption profiles (generic finite investment game); outsider alone supports member standard.
- **R13:** optimizing consumers with fulfilled network benefits, common economic basis for canonical \(p_i=1-Q+G_i,\ CS=Q^2/2\) and differentiated price games with \(p=\nabla U+G,\ CS=U-q^\top\nabla U\). This is an **admissible welfare accounting class**, not a novelty theorem in consumer choice.
- **G2/G3:** national-welfare incidence decomposition and a non-monotone *integrated sufficient bound* that can be verified from actual price/quantity/network responses without assuming the endpoint ranking. Standard accounting/integration mathematics is known.
- **R15:** exact original Cournot witness persists across nonempty \(C^2\) function-space neighborhoods of nonlinear inverse demand. Classical perturbation/strong monotonicity ensures strict-margin preservation; **nonlinear function-space breadth is real**, mathematical continuity method not new.
- **R16:** independent, consumer-consistent differentiated Bertrand price game: 19 distinct product-market continuations, full rational global unilateral price-deviation audit (211 candidate prices) and all product exit/entry configurations (171 inequality checks), unique positive equilibrium; 8 SU / 64 SW firm adoption profiles verified; exact G1 and G3 inequalities and SU-to-IS national preference reversal; nonlinear-quartic Bertrand example. This is a **substantive generality demonstration**.
- **Qualification:** R15 original Cournot and R16 Bertrand witnesses use **different parameters and differentiation structures**. An earlier R10 identical-parameter Cournot/Bertrand assertion was retracted after its incompatible consumer-surplus convention was discovered. R16 generality is *model-class conditional*, not a universal sign theorem.

## 4. Smallest possible distinctive theorem claim — what's actually new if not absorbed

The strongest claim must not be “endogenous standards”, “unilateral compatibility”, “Bertrand included”, “consumer-consistent pass-through”, “three-country coalition size”, or “private standards can substitute for public agreements”. Those each have direct precedent.

**Joint mechanism / conditional selection theorem (R16):** Within one optimizing-consumer network-expectations platform, and without fixing a quantity/price conduct FOC, a set of *primitive-checkable market-response and private-investment inequalities* jointly suffices for a **uniquely selected post-formation outsider-only private standard-support investment** and a **reversal of the very same member country's ranking of exclusionary regional versus all-country international standards**. The cross-stage link is that outsider support erodes national member-market protection while the members' disadvantage in outsider markets survives. Strictly nonempty successful environments are certified under **Cournot and Bertrand**, each with its own open neighborhood, and with nonlinear buyer-demand examples. Some differentiated settings FAIL (old R5).

**Formal result / novelty separation:**
1. The **finite adoption-G1** condition alone = elementary Nash/dominance, not new.
2. The **incidence identity / finite Riemann sum-G3** alone = welfare accounting and integration, not new.
3. The *coupled sign-feasible parameterization* plus government preference reversal after a purely private post-treaty investment, proved **for two distinct competition modes sharing a consumer foundation** = the remaining potentially distinct applied-IO theoretical claim.
4. The **\(C^2\) / \(C^3\) regularity neighborhoods** = technical model generality, not a discovery of new equilibrium existence mathematics. R16 C3 *global price-game uniqueness under every sufficiently small utility perturbation* has not yet received the same explicit uniform price-strategy bound proof as the exact quadratic benchmark and must be labeled **CONDITIONAL / PROOF-GATE**.
5. **Coalition stability** under the original full-bypass partition remains a secondary application, not automatically generalized when adopting R16 differentiable prices/partial adaptation.

The paper must state explicitly that **private adaptation is a hypothetical continuation used to compare welfare under unchanged formal institutions**. The observed change in national *ranking* does NOT establish a temporal event where governments actually rewrite/renegotiate the treaty after firm investment. Original Proposition/T2 blocking application is separately qualified. Do not claim “causal effect of exogenous firm intervention” when adoption is a strategic endogenous action.

### Supplemental primary-text correction: Maggi–Mrázová 2024 makes the novelty margin appreciably narrower

**2024 full-text source inspected:** https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2024-12/20241218-RegCoop.pdf, pp. 8–11 (government first chooses an exact emissions/product standard; firm chooses served markets and prices; fixed cost per variety, including one versus two standards); pp. 13–17 (Propositions 1–2 spontaneous harmonization and agreement-induced diversification/harmonization); pp. 30–34 (Propositions 6–7 intra-industry trade, coordination-only role, and Remark 5).

Important exact details:
1. In its one-way-trade benchmark, when the two governments choose the **same** product standard, the firm pays one fixed fee \(F\) to supply both markets, but when standards differ the firm must pay **\(2F\)** to supply both. The firm is **an optimizing post-government actor** that also chooses price and whether to enter/export. Thus (i) standards as shared fixed compliance costs, (ii) firm adaptation after public policy, and (iii) private incentives influencing formal agreements are already represented.
2. **Prop. 1:** sufficiently similar preferred regulations relative to \(F\) can induce spontaneous harmony, independent of agreement. **Prop. 2:** an agreement itself may *encourage harmonization or diversification* conditional on the regulatory-preference gaps and fee. Those are much stronger antecedents for institutional-feedback logic than a casual “private adaptation vs public agreements” abstract.
3. **Prop. 6 and 7 + Remark 5:** under intra-industry trade, multiple equilibria and a coordination-only role for formal agreements; in the linear subcase the agreement can be a force for *diversification*, not harmonization. The original R7 generic statement “regulatory agreements may not be necessary” **understates** this close overlap.
