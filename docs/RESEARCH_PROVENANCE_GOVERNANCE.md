## Provenance verdict

```text
BASELINE-MODEL + EXTENSION-IDEA
```

The available pre-AI undergraduate research contains the baseline three-country standards framework and explicitly identifies firm-level choice of foreign standards as a future extension. It does not contain the current endogenous private-adoption model, selective-erosion mechanism, coalition-stability analysis, or current theorem chain.

## Three-layer provenance

### Layer A — pre-AI baseline model

The earlier work already contained the following elements in substantive form:

- three countries and one domestic firm per country;
- segmented national product markets;
- Cournot quantity competition;
- heterogeneous consumer willingness to pay;
- compatibility/network benefits;
- a foreign-standard incompatibility/technology-gap unit cost;
- separate national standards, a two-country regional standardization union, and international standardization;
- government evaluation based on national consumer surplus plus worldwide profit of the domestic firm.

These are inherited baseline ingredients, not current-paper novelty claims.

### Layer B — pre-AI extension idea

The earlier work's conclusion proposes analyzing a setting in which firms themselves can choose to produce under another country's standard, so that firms as well as governments can choose whether to join a compatibility network.

The correct provenance statement is therefore that the **idea of endogenous firm-level standard choice predates generative AI**. The earlier work did not solve that extension or specify the current fixed-cost/support-set technology.

### Layer C — current formalization and results

The current paper newly formalizes or develops, relative to the earlier thesis:

- fixed adoption cost `F` per additional formal standard;
- standard-specific support decisions and support sets;
- the used-standard rule;
- formal versus effective compatibility;
- private adoption that changes technological support without changing formal coalition membership;
- the endogenous private-adoption equilibrium;
- all five three-country formal partitions;
- strict coalition blocking and a stability correspondence;
- the selective-erosion/private-circumvention mechanism;
- fixed-cost-dependent coalition-stability reversals;
- the low-`F` multistandarding branch;
- the current theorem/proposition chain.

## Textual and mathematical reuse rule

Sharing primitives, notation concepts, or standard Cournot first-order conditions is not itself textual self-reuse. The current paper must nevertheless avoid unacknowledged reuse of long prose passages, figures, tables, or proof text from the thesis.

The present audit establishes structural overlap and theorem-level distinction. It does **not** claim that a forensic machine-similarity scan of every sentence in both documents has been performed. Any discovered substantial verbatim or close-paraphrase reuse must be assessed separately under the publisher's text-recycling rules.
