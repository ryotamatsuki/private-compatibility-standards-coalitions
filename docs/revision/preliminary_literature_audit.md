# R1 Preliminary Literature and Theorem-Absorption Audit

## Status

**PRELIMINARY — SUFFICIENT FOR R2 DESIGN, NOT A FINAL NOVELTY CERTIFICATE**

This file updates the old standards-specific literature positioning for the new research question. It is deliberately broader than institutional labels such as "standards coalition".

The R2 novelty test asks whether the proposed result is already a standard theorem about fixed-cost adoption, economies of scope, or compatibility choice once the institutional vocabulary is stripped away.

## Strong antecedents already in the old project

The historical claim-control file already identifies close overlap in:

- Gandal & Shy (2001): government standards policy, network effects, conversion costs, standardization unions;
- Takarada et al. (2020): national/regional/multilateral standards, Cournot, coalition stability, fixed multi-standard production costs;
- Kawabata & Takarada (2021): regional versus multilateral harmonization;
- Farrell & Saloner (1992): converters/private workarounds;
- Doğanoglu & Wright (2006): multihoming;
- Buccella, Fanti & Gori (2023): endogenous compatibility, Cournot, network effects, quasi-fixed compatibility costs and one-way compatibility;
- Schmidt & Steingress (2022): costly firm adoption of harmonized standards;
- Maggi & Mrázová (working-paper line): fixed costs of regulatory diversity and spontaneous harmonization.

These already rule out novelty claims based merely on fixed compatibility costs, endogenous compatibility, one-way compatibility, or the coexistence of private and public routes to compatibility.

## R1 source refresh and new parent-class risks

### Buccella, Fanti & Gori (2023)

*Strategic product compatibility in network industries*, Journal of Economics 140, 141–168. DOI: `10.1007/s00712-023-00834-x`.

Publisher evidence confirms a Cournot compatibility-decision game with network consumption externalities and quasi-fixed compatibility costs, including multiple compatibility regimes and an explanation for one-way compatibility.

**R2 implication:** one-way compatibility/adoption is not itself a new theorem target.

### Manenti & Somma (2008)

*One-Way Compatibility, Two-Way Compatibility and Entry in Network Industries*, International Journal of the Economics of Business 15(3), 301–322.

Publisher issue metadata confirms the article and one-way/two-way compatibility framing.

**R2 implication:** asymmetric compatibility outcomes predate this project; the project must explain the institutional/scope mechanism and its downstream government incentive, not claim one-way compatibility as novelty.

### Farrell & Simcoe (2012)

*Four Paths to Compatibility*, in *The Oxford Handbook of the Digital Economy*, pp. 34–58. DOI: `10.1093/oxfordhb/9780195397840.013.0002`.

Oxford's abstract explicitly treats alternative ways to achieve compatibility, including decentralized/private routes and formal standard-setting routes.

**R2 implication:** "private compatibility can substitute for formal standardization" is established at a broad conceptual level. The revision needs a conditional prediction about when private adaptation strengthens versus weakens incentives for broader formal coordination.

### Cho & McCardle (2009)

*The Adoption of Multiple Dependent Technologies*, Operations Research 57(1), 157–169. DOI: `10.1287/opre.1080.0534`.

INFORMS describes economies or diseconomies of scope in fixed adoption costs as creating economic dependence among technology-adoption decisions.

**R2 implication:** a theorem that merely says a shared fixed cost makes broader-scope adoption more attractive risks direct absorption by generic technology-adoption theory.

### Gorman (1985)

*Conditions for Economies of Scope in the Presence of Fixed Costs*, RAND Journal of Economics 16(3), 431–436. DOI: `10.2307/2555569`.

Preliminary bibliographic search identifies this as a parent-class source on fixed costs and economies of scope.

**Verification status:** bibliographic/abstract-level candidate for theorem-absorption mapping; full theorem mapping remains R2 work.

### Matutes & Régibeau (1989)

*Standardization Across Markets and Entry*, Journal of Industrial Economics 37(4).

This is a direct warning that "standardization across markets" and strategic effects of serving multiple submarkets have old antecedents.

**Verification status:** close structural candidate; exact theorem absorption must be checked in R2/R7.

### Maggi & Mrázová

*Harmonization...What Else? The Role for International Regulatory Agreements*, NBER Working Paper 33318, DOI `10.3386/w33318`.

NBER states that firms incur fixed costs of regulatory diversity and that harmonization can arise without an agreement; the paper asks what role remains for formal regulatory agreements.

**R2/R3 implication:** the broad question "does private cost-saving adaptation substitute for formal cooperation?" is already occupied. The prospective distinction must be the conditions under which asymmetric private adaptation changes the *relative political incentive* for broader formal coordination, including failure conditions.

## Application-neutral parent classes to search in R2/R7

1. fixed-cost economies of scope;
2. multi-market entry with common sunk costs;

## Preliminary novelty judgment

The old classification `DISTINCT BUT NARROW` remains a useful historical description, but it is no longer enough to support a new theorem claim.

For R2, the presumptive classification is:

`THEOREM NOVELTY UNRESOLVED — HIGH ABSORPTION RISK`

A G1 result can count as substantive new theory only if it does more than:

- restate "adopt iff net benefit is positive";
- restate "a common fixed cost creates economies of scope";
- reproduce an established one-way compatibility equilibrium with standards labels.

A potentially surviving contribution would need to connect formal-standard scope, endogenous product-market profit effects, and asymmetric adoption incentives in a way that yields a nontrivial success/failure condition not directly inherited from the parent class.
