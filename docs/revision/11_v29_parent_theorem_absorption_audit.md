## 1. Closest predecessor: Ding, Ko & Shen (2022), Economic Modelling 116, 105989

Original: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264999322002322
DOI 10.1016/j.econmod.2022.105989.

**Proved as stated in accessible publisher abstract/introduction (exact numbered propositions not accessible):** Three symmetric competing two-sided platforms with single-homing users and multihoming content providers choose compatibility independently, then compete in membership prices, then users/providers select platforms. The authors characterize generically unique **perfect coalition-proof Nash** compatibility outcomes. Partial compatibility can be the unique outcome; with relative network effects sufficiently high, rising compatibility costs can switch directly from full compatibility to incompatibility; partial compatibility can maximize CS/total surplus. They explicitly distinguish individual vs collective compatibility decisions and show policies need not always subsidize compatibility. Section 5 changes welfare objective, transportation costs, providers' joining cost, compatibility formation process.

**Application-neutral parent class:** endogenous private compatibility networks + profitable coalition deviations + endogenous two-sided price equilibria + welfare.

**Attempted mapping to R9:** platform compatibility choices ↔ private standard adoption; coalition-proof platform deviations ↔ formal-government coalition blocking (FAILS mapping: agents/payoffs/contractual membership and available deviations are different). Platform revenue/fees are not nation-specific government CS+domestic worldwide firm profit. R9 has government choice of SW/SU/IS followed by unilateral firm adaptation, not platform coalitions as the institutional actor.

**Absorption:** component-level and private-compatibility coalition-stability insight `SUBSTANTIALLY OCCUPIED`; full R9 institutional theorem `NOT DIRECTLY MAPPED` on current evidence; proposed D government-vs-private-coalition wedge `UNRESOLVED`. Bare existence of differing firm/government rankings is trivial under nonidentical welfare/payoffs, and fails the editor's significance test.

## 2. Closest predecessor: Llanes & Poblete (2020), Journal of Industrial Economics 68:270–297

Published abstract: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/joie.12207
2015 preprint (45 pages, VERSION LIMITED): https://www.tse-fr.eu/sites/default/files/medias/doc/conf/sic/conf%202015/llanes.pdf
DOI 10.1111/joie.12207.

**Exact accessible WORKING-PAPER propositions:**
- WP Proposition 1 (PDF p.10): stable allocation exists under the authors' standard-sponsoring game, with reactive allocation representation, duopoly-standards restriction and stated uniqueness simplification.
- WP Proposition 5 (PDF p.20): IF a connected first-best allocation exists, ALL stable allocations are first-best, under unrestricted interconnected participation and feasible sharing/payments. This is not a general welfare/core theorem absent connectivity, sharing, and the authors' profit/welfare conditions.
- WP Section 6 + Conclusion: ex-ante agreements can lower welfare in standards wars even when profits rise, because sponsor incentives and social welfare are misaligned, unlike certain monopoly-standard settings.
- WP Section 8 pp.21–23 AUTHOR-STATED: bargaining costs; easy-to-substitute technologies vs difficult-to-reverse choices; innovation incentives. Section 8 also contains model internal results, so not every item is unstudied. Verify any statement against the 2020 journal version before attributing it to the published conclusion.

**Attempted mapping:** firm technology sponsoring coalition ↔ formal government standards coalition (FAILS actor mapping); expected producer revenues and permissible transfers ↔ government-specific CS + worldwide domestic profit without transfers (FAILS payoff/transfer mapping); technical standard war/mandated standard ↔ public SW/SU/IS (only partial institutional analogy). The abstract efficiency reversal is ALREADY explained as firm-vs-social welfare misalignment. Thus mere welfare or coalition preference differences are not novel.

**Absorption:** firm sponsorship coalition existence / transfers-connectivity welfare: `PRIOR THEOREM`; public-vs-private discrepancy or irreversibility per se: `PARTIALLY ABSORBED / HIGH RISK`; full government/private investment renegotiation game: `UNRESOLVED`.

## 3. Closest predecessor: Klimenko (2009), International Review of Economics & Finance 18:539–551

Publisher original: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1059-0560%2808%2900124-X
DOI 10.1016/j.iref.2008.09.015.

**Stated publisher abstract/introduction:** Two-country home-market framework with domestic/foreign producers of imperfectly compatible products, consumer network effects and heterogeneous demand. Home government jointly chooses import tariffs and minimum inter-product compatibility standard affecting foreign producer. Foreign government chooses export policy and incentives to enhance compatibility (e.g. export tax or supportive policy). Socially suboptimal stringency or protectionist compatibility policy can result.

**Attempted mapping:** government policy on compatibility ↔ public formal coalition arrangements (only partial; unilateral regulation and trade taxes differ from group-membership choice and blocking); firm costly compatibility ↔ private adaptation (occupied technology); country-national welfare ↔ country government payoff (some correspondence).

**Absorption:** generic government-firm compatibility-policy interaction `ALREADY STUDIED`; merely subsidizing compatibility is NOT new. Coalition-dependent, forward-looking investment-induced stability under multiple governments: `UNRESOLVED` and requires new theorem beyond a tax-policy reskin.

## 4. Critical newly identified PARENT CLASS for proposal B: dynamic cooperation, commitment and endogenous investment

A. Battaglini & Harstad (2016), _Participation and Duration of Environmental Agreements_, Journal of Political Economy 124(1):160–204, DOI 10.1086/684478. Original: https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/684478
- Dynamic country participation, green investment, agreement duration and contractibility.
- If complete investment contracts, freeriding can keep coalition small; if investments non-contractible, hold-up on renegotiation can make large coalitions more attractive due to long-duration commitment, even first-best in specific conditions.
- **Direct parent of proposed generic claim**: 'uncontractible sunk investment and renegotiation may change coalition stability'—HIGH RISK, not a novel question by itself.
- Verified at abstract level only; proof-level mapping UNRESOLVED.

B. _A dynamic climate negotiation game achieving full cooperation_ (JEBO 214, 2023:657–669), DOI 10.1016/j.jebo.2023.08.019, original https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268123003086.
- Repeated country membership, optional exit/re-entry, transfers and renegotiation-proof/state-dependent SPE. Irreversible memberships can generate convergence to grand coalition in MPE; reversible case may sustain small-coalition outcome.
- Strong parent threat to any blanket 'irreversibility promotes/weakens broader alliance' sign.

C. Karp & Sakamoto (2021), _Sober optimism and the formation of international environmental agreements_, Journal of Economic Theory 197:105321, DOI 10.1016/j.jet.2021.105321. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022053121001381
- Repeated negotiations, MPE/Markov chain of transient coalitions, large coalition absorbing state. State dynamics and bargaining are OLD ingredients.

D. Cabral & Salant (2014), _Evolving technologies and standards regulation_, IJIO 36:48–56, DOI 10.1016/j.ijindorg.2013.07.006. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167718713000805
- Dynamic R&D and standards policy: mandating one standard can reduce incentives to invest/innovate, potentially lower welfare; separate standards can sustain investment.

E. Llanes (2024), _Innovation incentives in technical standards_, IJIO 93:103046, DOI 10.1016/j.ijindorg.2024.103046. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167718724000018
- Four-stage R&D -> standards inclusion negotiation -> licensing -> user adoption, with hold-up, complementary innovations, policy price caps/patent pools/R&D agreements.
- A 'firms invest first, negotiate standard later' structure is itself decisively occupied.

F. Eichner & Kollenbach (2022), _Participation and Duration of Environmental Agreements: Investment Lags Matter_, JAERE DOI 10.1086/721095. https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jaerec/doi10.1086-721095.html
- Timing/long vs short lag technology changes investment hold-up and coalition participation; mere dynamic investment timing extension already occupied.

**Aggregate parent verdict for B:** 'investment irreversibility + formal coalition re-negotiation' as a generic idea is materially preempted by these papers. No asserted direct absorption of a public-standards-specific two-layer equilibrium, because exact theorem mappings (especially Battaglini-Harstad) have not been completed.

## 5. Strongest possible NONTRIVIAL residual question (HYPOTHESIS ONLY)

**B2. Firms bear private, not governmental, noncontractible standards adaptation investments; when a government agreement is renegotiable, does the divergence between firm/private incentive and national continuation welfare produce an equilibrium institutional response *not derivable* from dynamic-IEA hold-up, fixed-cost adoption or existing R9 residual-rent conditions?**

Potential falsifiable contribution target: a primitive-condition characterization of whether an initial regional agreement induces firm investment that ex post makes multilateral renegotiation self-enforcing; compare the no-renegotiation, complete-contract, and government-investor nested benchmarks **holding welfare and feasible deviations fixed**. Pre-register null: identical predictions/one-line specialization of Battaglini-Harstad; or straightforward CS/firm-profit wedges without genuinely new strategic feedback => NO-GO. Only an exact, substantively surprising reversal, impossibility, sharp boundary or additional endogenous feedback could merit continuation.

**D2. Separation of private compatibility coalitions from formal government coalitions** cannot be claimed novel merely because private coalition-proof Nash and government core/blocking give different stable sets. That follows generically once players, transfer feasibility, and welfare differ. A credible D2 result must characterize a nontrivial *coincidence/decoupling boundary*, a coordination failure or an implementation impossibility from incentive-compatible firm choices, compared explicitly with Ding and Llanes–Poblete and R9, and with all coalition deviations recomputed.
