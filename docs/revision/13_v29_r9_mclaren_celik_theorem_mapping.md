## A. Celik, Karabay & McLaren (2020)

Title: Fast-track authority: A hold-up interpretation, Journal of International Economics 127, 103392, DOI 10.1016/j.jinteco.2020.103392.
Author university accepted version: https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/24989/1/FTA_new_FINAL_10Sep2020.pdf
Reading level: 40-page accepted manuscript, sections 2–9 and exact Propositions 12 and 13 inspected. Article states that proofs of Proposition 2 onwards are in their 2018 working paper; proof-by-proof independent replication NOT completed. Accepted version may differ from typeset final.

Timing: US–Mexico negotiations precede private exporters' sunk investments, followed by US Congressional amendment at ratification if fast-track commitment absent. ROO restrictiveness R, Mexican precommitted number of export firms n_tilde, tariff tau, input network strength eta, beta and rho determine national welfare and amendment incentives.

Proposition 12, accepted PDF printed p.31 (PDF page 33/40): when beta<rho, tau sufficiently small and eta small (weak backward and forward production linkages), the committed R* is strictly positive and makes Mexico indifferent to no agreement. Without FT, US ex post increases R above R* after Mexican firms invest; Mexico is worse off than with no agreement; hence it would not initiate talks without commitment. The result is a precise hold-up effect, not merely an intuitive example.

Proposition 13, printed p.32 (PDF page 34/40): when beta<rho, tau sufficiently small, eta close to 1 and beta close to rho, precommitted Mexican exporting investments strengthen its outside option; Mexico may demand R below R* ex post instead. The direction of hold-up reverses. Thus private sunk investment improving/damaging governments' bargaining leverage is prior art.

Source-author future/informal suggestion: Conclusion §9 discusses possible intermediate input-linkage intensity where neither side has hold-up, explicitly as a conceivable case, NOT a proved necessary-and-sufficient boundary. Do not promote this to a verified open theorem. Footnote 19 (accepted PDF printed p.12) mentions Mexican subsidy addressing entry externalities as possible extension; it is not a newly discovered gap without forward checks.

Primary source location: accepted PDF pp. 28–34, eq. (35)–(37), Proposition 10–13, Conclusion §9; exact references above.

## D. Exact R9 nested quick kill

Certified R9: Delta0 = W_SU(no bypass)-W_IS = E-D.
Certified R9: Delta1 = W_SU(outsider bypass;d)-W_IS = -D+ResidualRent(d,v), where ResidualRent(d,v)=d*((5-4*v)*d+2*(1-4*v))/(32*(1-v)^2).
Therefore Delta1-Delta0 = ResidualRent(d,v)-E, already R3/R7. Both regimes holding installed private adoption state fixed and bearing same irreversible payment F yield identical difference because F cancels. Python/SymPy smoke check confirmed that expression and d/dF(Delta1)=0.
This is a NONRESULT about additive constant sunk expenditure, not a new theorem. Its scope explicitly excludes endogenous changes in installed state, state-dependent reuse/abandonment, differential post-policy switching costs, or history-dependent policy constraints.
