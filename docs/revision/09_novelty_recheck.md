## Findings

The refresh again located the major parent classes already controlling R7:

- Farrell–Simcoe: decentralized compatibility, negotiation, leadership, converters/multihoming;
- Manenti–Somma and later compatibility models: one-way and two-way compatibility;
- Gandal–Shy / Takarada-related standards-policy work: government standards, standardization unions and regional/multilateral arrangements;
- fixed-cost adoption/economies-of-scope work;
- Schmidt–Steingress: costly firm adoption of harmonized standards;
- Maggi–Mrázová (2025 working-paper line): fixed costs of regulatory diversity, spontaneous harmonization, and the residual role of formal agreements;
- Casella's standards-coalition line: private groups, technology/market conditions, and cross-border standards alliances.

## Claim consequences

R9 retains the R7 novelty hierarchy:

1. **Principal surviving contribution:** model-specific conditional government-incentive partition generated by equilibrium residual rent.
2. **Supporting contribution:** incomplete adaptation and preference reversal coexist on a nonempty open set with a selection-free private continuation.
3. **Scope contribution:** zero-network, singleton-network, differentiated-demand, blocking-rule, and asymmetry tests identify explicit failure/robustness boundaries.
4. **Not novelty:** one-way compatibility, fixed-cost scope, generic private-vs-public compatibility, standards coalitions, or strict-vs-weak blocking as abstract phenomena.

The manuscript must continue to avoid “first”, general compatibility, demand-system independence, competition-form independence, or general-harmonization claims.

## R9 novelty judgment

The revised paper remains publishable only as a **conditional applied-theory mechanism paper**. R9 finds no basis to upgrade Route B to Route A and no new evidence requiring downgrade to Route C/D.
