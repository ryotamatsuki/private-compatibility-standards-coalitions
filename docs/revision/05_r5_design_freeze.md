## 1. Why a new demand system is unavoidable

The canonical Stage-8 inverse demand is

[
p_i=1+g_i-sum_j q_j,
]

which is a perfect-substitutes boundary case. Direct Bertrand competition with the same homogeneous-good demand is degenerate for the intended interior comparison. R5 therefore uses one differentiated linear-demand environment and solves **both Cournot and Bertrand in that same environment**. This separates:

1. the effect of changing the demand microfoundation relative to the canonical paper; and
2. the effect of switching the strategic variable from quantity to price within the R5 environment.

Any difference between the canonical paper and R5 cannot automatically be attributed to Cournot versus Bertrand. Only the within-R5 Cournot/Bertrand comparison can support a competition-mode statement.

## 3. Costs, formal regimes, and private adoption

All non-demand primitives are inherited from the canonical model unless explicitly stated here.

- Three countries and one domestic firm per country.
- Three segmented national markets.
- Symmetric market masses (m_1=m_2=m_3=1).
- Formal regimes: SW, any two-country SU, and IS.
- Native/local standard or supported standard: marginal cost (0).
- Unsupported local standard: marginal adaptation cost (c>0).
- Additional-standard adoption cost: one fixed cost (F>0) per adopted formal standard.
- Used-standard rule: if a firm supports the local formal standard, it uses it; otherwise it uses its baseline standard.

## 4. Two strategic-variable versions of the same R5 demand

For each market configuration, R5 solves two games from the same (M_G(v)), cost vector, and regime.

### R5-C: differentiated Cournot comparator

Firms choose quantities. Firm (i)'s market profit is

[
pi_i=(p_i-r_i)q_i.
]

This is **not** the canonical Cournot model; it is the Cournot game induced by the frozen differentiated R5 demand.

### R5-B: differentiated Bertrand target

Firms choose prices. Direct demand is

[
q=M_G(v)^{-1}(mathbf 1-p).
]

Firm (i)'s market profit is

[
pi_i=(p_i-r_i)q_i.
]

Only interior equilibria with strictly positive quantities are used for headline comparisons. Exit/corner cases are checked separately and cannot be silently ignored.

For every result R5 must determine the actual admissible subset satisfying:

- (M_G(v)) positive definite / direct demand well defined;
- the relevant Cournot or Bertrand equilibrium exists and is unique;
- all quantities used in an interior formula are strictly positive;
- prices and markups are finite;
- consumer surplus is well defined.

At (v=0), boundary results may be stated as boundary extensions. At any exit boundary, the interior formula stops; it is not extrapolated through the corner.

## 7. Search and proof budget

The model above is the only R5 specification.

Permitted after solving begins:

- algebraic simplification;
- correction of coding or derivation mistakes;
- exact root isolation;
- exact sign proofs;
- a finite numerical scan used only to locate candidate sign regions or counterexamples;
- derivation of analytic conditions suggested by the frozen model.

Not permitted:

- changing (gamma=1/2) because a result fails;
- changing the network rule;
- changing the government objective;
- changing the fixed-cost scope;
- adding asymmetry;
- adding a residual-cost parameter to rescue the result;
- trying a second differentiated-demand family;
- changing the welfare measure after observing results.

If a numerical scan fails to find a region and no analytic nonexistence proof is obtained, the status is

`UNRESOLVED WITHIN SEARCH BUDGET`.

If a failure is proved for this model, the conclusion is model-specific.
