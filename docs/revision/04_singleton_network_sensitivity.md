# R4 Singleton-Network Sensitivity Audit

## Status

**SPECIFICATION DEPENDENCE IDENTIFIED — MINIMAL S1 ALTERNATIVE CERTIFIED**

This file is a sensitivity analysis. It does not modify the Stage-8 canonical model and does not authorize a production-manuscript theorem.

---

## 1. Canonical primitive being audited

The canonical consumer utility and inverse demand are

\[
u+g_i^k-p_i^k,
\qquad
p_i^k=1+g_i^k-\sum_jq_j^k.
\]

For a compatibility group \(G_i^k\),

\[
g_i^k
=
v\sum_{j\in G_i^k}q_j^k
\quad\text{if }|G_i^k|\ge2,
\]

whereas

\[
g_i^k=0
\quad\text{if }|G_i^k|=1.
\]

Thus a singleton receives no network term at all, including no own-demand contribution.

This is the exact primitive behind the “singleton compatibility group has no network benefit” property.

---

## 2. Canonical blocks that depend on the exception

The exception does not affect complete compatibility: under IS, and in the R3 member market after outsider adoption, all three firms are in the same non-singleton group.

It does affect:

1. **SU member market.** The excluded outsider is a singleton. Its quantity/profit block \(B\) changes directly; \(A\) and \(K_M\) change through strategic interaction.
2. **SU outsider market.** The native outsider is a singleton. \(D\) changes directly; \(C\) and \(K_O\) change strategically.
3. **SW.** Every firm is a singleton. In the canonical model this is why the SW Cournot blocks contain no \(v\).
4. **One-adopter states.** The remaining non-adopter is a singleton, so reverse-adoption incentives depend on the singleton rule.
5. **Pre-adoption welfare decomposition.** Both \(\mathscr E\) and \(\mathscr D\) inherit the changed pre-adoption blocks.

The R3 residual-rent function generated by the **all-compatible post-adoption member market** is not directly generated by the singleton exception.

---

## 3. Minimal alternative S1

To test whether the exception is merely a normalization, fix one alternative before inspecting its success.

S1 removes only the cardinality exception:

\[
\boxed{
g_i^k
=
v\sum_{j\in G_i^k}q_j^k
\quad\text{for every nonempty compatibility group, including a singleton.}
}
\]

For a singleton this becomes

\[
g_i^k=vq_i^k.
\]

No new parameter is introduced.

Economic interpretation: the installed-base/network term includes the firm's own users even when no rival product is compatible. Compatibility with rivals adds their quantities to the same network term.

S1 keeps:

- the same utility representation;
- the same inverse-demand construction;
- the same marginal adaptation cost \(c\);
- the same adoption cost \(F\);
- the same government objective;
- the same consumer-surplus formula \(CS=X^2/2\);
- the same formal partitions and timing.

At \(v=0\), S1 maps exactly back to the canonical zero-network boundary.

S1 is one economically coherent minimum perturbation. It is **not** asserted to be the unique sensible alternative.

## 4. S1 Cournot blocks

Let

\[
K(v)=3v^2-6v+2.
\]

In an SU member market, the two bloc firms are compatible and the outsider is singleton. The unique interior FOC solution is

\[
q_M^{S1}
=
\frac{1+c-2v}{2K(v)},
\]

\[
q_B^{S1}
=
\frac{1-3v-3c(1-v)}{2K(v)}.
\]

In an SU outsider market,

\[
q_C^{S1}
=
\frac{1-2v-2c(1-v)}{2K(v)},
\]

\[
q_D^{S1}
=
\frac{1+2c-3v}{2K(v)}.
\]

Because own output now belongs to the network term even for singleton firms, the relevant operating-profit blocks take the common form

\[
\pi_i=(1-v)q_i^2
\]

at the interior FOC.

For the member-market consumer surplus,

\[
K_M^{S1}
=
\frac12
\left(2q_M^{S1}+q_B^{S1}\right)^2.
\]

The S1 formulas are independently reconstructed from the FOCs in the R4 verification script.

## 5. Initial SU advantage under S1

Define

\[
\Phi_{S1}
=
K_M^{S1}
+2(1-v)(q_M^{S1})^2
+(1-v)(q_C^{S1})^2
-W^{IS}.
\]

Its denominator is

\[
32(1-v)^2K(v)^2>0
\]

on the original canonical feasible domain.

Using the same two fixed-\(c\) feasibility cases as the canonical proof, transform the feasible \(v\)-interval to \(x>0\) with

\[
v=\frac{U(c)x}{1+x}.
\]

After clearing positive denominators, **all coefficients of the transformed S1 numerator are strictly negative** in both ranges

\[
0<c\le\frac19
\]

and

\[
\frac19<c<\frac13.
\]

The coefficient signs are certified by exact endpoint signs and/or Sturm root counts in `check_r4_network_role.py`.

Therefore there is no feasible zero and no positive region:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{S1}(c,v)<0
}
\]

throughout

\[
0<v<\frac14,
\qquad
0<c<\frac{1-3v}{3(1-v)}.
\]

This is a direct specification-sensitivity result: the canonical pre-adoption SU advantage does not survive this minimal self-network alternative.

## 6. Reciprocal disadvantage under S1

Let

\[
\mathscr D_{S1}=P-C_{S1}.
\]

Exact factorization gives a sign boundary that differs from the canonical one. On the original canonical feasible domain, one factor is strictly negative and the denominator has fixed sign, leaving

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr D_{S1}>0
\iff
4c(1-v)^2>v^2
\iff
c>\frac{v^2}{4(1-v)^2}.
}
\]

The canonical boundary was

\[
c>\frac{v}{4(1-v)}.
\]

Therefore the singleton convention changes not only the level of welfare but also the primitive sign boundary for reciprocal disadvantage.

## 7. One-way adoption under S1

The disappearance of the initial SU advantage does **not** imply that private one-way adoption disappears.

For S1 define, consistently with the re-solved blocks,

\[
T_A^{S1}=P-B_{S1},
\]

\[
T_U^{S1}=A_{S1}-C_{S1},
\]

and

\[
T_W^{S1}=A_{S1}-S_{S1},
\]

where \(S_{S1}\) is the re-solved foreign-firm SW profit under singleton self-network benefits.

At the exact point

\[
(c,v)=\left(\frac1{10},\frac6{25}\right),
\]

the certified values are approximately

\[
T_A^{S1}=0.0812801,
\qquad
T_U^{S1}=0.0880927,
\qquad
T_W^{S1}=0.105289.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\max\{T_U^{S1},T_A^{S1},T_W^{S1}\}
<
2T_A^{S1}.
\]

Thus there is still a positive common-\(F\) interval supporting outsider adoption while shutting down the relevant reverse/SW adoption incentives.

At this same point,

\[
\Phi_{S1}<0,
\qquad
\mathscr D_{S1}>0.
\]

So S1 cleanly separates the private-adoption mechanism from the initial government preference for SU.

## 8. Relation to R3 residual rent

After the outsider adopts the bloc standard in R3, all three firms in the member market are compatible. S1 changes only the singleton rule, so that all-compatible post-adoption market is unchanged.

Consequently the R3 residual-rent formula remains

\[
R(d,v)
=
\frac{
d[(5-4v)d+2(1-4v)]
}{
32(1-v)^2
}.
\]

What changes under S1 is the **pre-adoption benchmark** from which erosion is evaluated. In particular, the initial SU advantage is absent on the old feasible domain.

Therefore “the residual-rent identity survives” and “the SU-to-IS ranking reversal survives” are different statements. Only the former survives S1 on the domain tested.

---

## 9. Audit conclusion

The canonical zero-benefit singleton rule is **not a pure normalization** for the headline Cournot microfoundation. It materially raises the relative value of multi-firm compatibility compared with fragmented/singleton configurations and is essential to the existence of the canonical initial-SU-preference region when compared with S1.

However, S1 does not show that:

- every alternative singleton-network model eliminates an SU advantage;
- one-way private adoption requires the canonical singleton rule;
- the R2 abstract scope mechanism fails;
- the R3 all-compatible residual-rent identity fails.

The certified statement is narrower:

> Under the pre-specified minimal S1 alternative that gives a singleton the same own-group network formula, the initial SU advantage disappears throughout the original canonical feasible domain, while one-way adoption remains possible at an exact interior witness.

Accordingly the broader theorem status is **CONDITIONAL**: specification dependence is established, but no universal theorem over all possible singleton-network formulations is claimed.
