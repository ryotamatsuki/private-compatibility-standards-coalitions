## 4. Editor-facing rejection paragraph (hypothetical)

Although the manuscript carefully derives regional-versus-multilateral welfare differences and demonstrates a genuine nonknife-edge adoption equilibrium, the main three-way classification appears to arise from a comparison of two pre-defined rents under one restrictive linear Cournot microfoundation. An alternative, economically defensible differentiated-demand specification eliminates the very initial regional preference needed for the central reversal. The manuscript does not yet establish why this partition changes our general understanding of standards organizations, rather than illustrating model-specific rent accounting. Moreover, its stronger coalition-stability comparison is largely confined to full adaptation, whereas the newly added mechanism focuses on incomplete cost relief. These issues raise a substantial question about whether the additional economic learning supports a full applied-theory article.

## 5. Potential affirmative editorial case (to be independently tested, not pre-certified)

The paper treats technological interoperability and public harmonization as distinct institution-level choices. It derives a concrete product-market rent that can cause private adaptation to increase, fail to change, or weaken governments' incentives for internationalization, with sufficient firm-stage adoption conditions imposed simultaneously rather than conditioning on an impossible private action. Its negative portability and coalition-concept tests identify what cannot be inferred from one-way adoption alone. The applied-theory value lies in a precisely bounded diagnosis of when private circumvention changes government-institution incentives, not a universal adoption or public cooperation theorem.
