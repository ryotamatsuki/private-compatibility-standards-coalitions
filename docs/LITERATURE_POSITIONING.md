# Literature Positioning and Claim Control

This document is a **claim-control file**, not a bibliography and not a substitute for source verification. It records the closest literatures that must constrain novelty claims when the manuscript is written.

Bibliographic metadata must be verified from original articles, publishers, author manuscripts, NBER/CEPR, or another official source before entering `references.bib`.

## Frozen Contribution Statement

Default central contribution statement:

> Private compatibility can destabilize an exclusionary formal standards coalition because firms can selectively circumvent the coalition's exclusionary benefits without providing reciprocal compatibility to coalition members.

Mechanism formulation:

> The paper identifies a selective-erosion mechanism: private compatibility removes the member-market exclusion surplus that supports a regional standards coalition while leaving coalition members' reciprocal disadvantage in the outsider market intact.

Mathematical representation:

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_i-\mathscr D_i
\longrightarrow
-\mathscr D_i
}
\]

A concise ranking-reversal formulation that is also permitted is:

> We identify a selective-erosion mechanism through which private compatibility choices can reverse governments' ranking between regional and multilateral standardization.

Do not strengthen these statements into a global “first” claim without a separate, explicit literature-verification stage.

## Mandatory Claim Restrictions

Do not write any of the following in the manuscript unless a later dedicated literature search explicitly overturns this control file:

> We are the first to combine governments and firm standard decisions.

> We are the first to show that compliance costs affect harmonization incentives.

> We are the first to show regional standards can block multilateral harmonization.

> We are the first to introduce fixed costs into standards competition.

> No previous paper considers private compatibility.

Also avoid softer variants that are substantively equivalent, such as “the literature has ignored private compatibility” or “existing work treats standards only as government choices,” unless source-by-source verification supports them.

## IJIO Positioning

For an IJIO submission, the manuscript must foreground the industrial-organization channel:

- strategic firm compatibility/adoption decisions;
- Cournot product-market competition;
- profit displacement generated by compatibility and exclusion;
- endogenous formal-coalition rents;
- feedback from private firm behavior to government policy incentives.

The key causal chain to communicate is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{firm compatibility choice}
\rightarrow
\text{product-market competition}
\rightarrow
\text{coalition rents}
\rightarrow
\text{government preference over formal standards regimes}
}
\]

The paper should not read primarily as a generic international-cooperation model with firms appended.

## Relationship to Network Economics

Network effects are part of the canonical Cournot standards model and can strengthen or alter formal coalition incentives. They are not the general source of the selective-erosion theorem.

Permitted positioning:

> The model accommodates network effects, but the selective-erosion mechanism does not require the paper to be framed as a new network-externality theorem.

Do not claim that network effects are necessary for the central mechanism.

## Technological Substitute / Political Complement Language

The permitted result is the binary cross-difference in the government's gain from multilateralization:

\[
G_i(1)-G_i(0)=\mathscr E_i>0.
\]

The phrase “technological substitute / political complement” is acceptable only when the text makes clear that:

- private compatibility narrows the effective technological difference between regional and multilateral formal standards; and
- private compatibility raises the government's incentive for multilateralization by eroding regional exclusion rents.

Do not claim continuous-action complementarity, general supermodularity, or a universal comparative-statics theorem.

## What the Paper Is Not Claiming

The frozen model does not establish robustness to:

- Bertrand competition;
- heterogeneous firms;
- transfers;
- continuous compatibility;
- installed bases;
- dynamics;
- endogenous innovation;
- four or more countries as a full model;
- alternative coalition solution concepts;
- empirical calibration.

These are outside the current model and must not be presented as verified robustness results.

## Literature Verification Protocol for the Next Stage

Before manuscript prose cites any item listed here:

1. locate an authoritative source;
2. verify authors, title, year/status, journal or working-paper series, volume/issue/pages if applicable, and DOI or stable identifier when available;
3. read enough of the original source to verify the claimed overlap;
4. record the citation in the future `references.bib`;
5. update this claim matrix if the verified source materially changes the comparison.

If the closest literature appears to absorb the selective-erosion mechanism itself, stop and report the issue rather than weakening the distinction silently.

## Single-Sentence Novelty Test

A compliant draft should be able to answer “What is new?” approximately as follows:

> Private compatibility can selectively eliminate the exclusionary benefit that sustains a regional standards coalition while leaving its members' reciprocal disadvantage intact, thereby reversing their preference toward multilateral harmonization.

If the draft instead relies on “we combine X and Y,” broad priority claims, network effects alone, market-size asymmetry, or low-\(F\) fixed-cost accounting, the positioning has drifted from the frozen contribution.
