\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider the manuscript ``Private Compatibility and the Stability of Standards Coalitions'' for publication in the \emph{International Journal of Industrial Organization}. The paper asks how firms' private ability to overcome standards incompatibility affects the stability of formal regional and international standards arrangements.

The model adds a post-coalition firm decision to a three-country Cournot standards environment. Firms can pay a fixed cost to support an additional standard after governments have selected the formal standards regime. This generates a selective-erosion mechanism: when the outsider to a regional standards union adopts the bloc standard, coalition members lose the exclusion advantage they previously enjoyed in member markets, while their reciprocal disadvantage in the outsider market remains. On a nonempty open set of product-market parameters, this change in continuation payoffs reverses governments' ranking between regional and international standardization. Regional standards unions are stable when private compatibility is sufficiently costly, whereas an intermediate compatibility-cost range makes international standardization uniquely stable.

The contribution is industrial-organizational in its causal channel. Strategic firm compatibility choices change product-market competition and coalition-specific rents, which in turn change governments' incentives over formal standards institutions. Relative to the closest regional-versus-multilateral standards models, the paper's distinction is therefore not the presence of another fixed cost; it is the endogenous post-coalition private-adoption stage and the directional outsider-bypass state through which firm conduct feeds back into formal coalition stability.

The manuscript is accompanied by a replication package containing the symbolic and numerical verification code and the scripts used to generate all figures and tables. No empirical data are used.
