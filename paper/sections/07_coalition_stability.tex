\section{Coalition-Stability Application}
\label{sec:coalition-stability}

The residual-rent result in Section~\ref{sec:selective-erosion} concerns government incentives. We now show one institutional consequence in the canonical full-bypass application. This section returns to $d=0$, so outsider adoption of the bloc standard yields complete compatibility and complete marginal-cost relief in member markets. The purpose is not to claim that every partial-adaptation point has the same stable set; that would require re-solving the outsider-government and deviation comparisons under $d>0$.

Let
\begin{equation}
\Omega_0
=
\left\{
(c,v):
\begin{array}{l}
0<v<1/4,\;
0<c<\dfrac{1-3v}{3(1-v)},\\[2mm]
W_M^{SU,N}>W^{IS},\;
P>C,\;
F_L<2T_A
\end{array}
\right\}.
\label{eq:omega-zero}
\end{equation}
R4 characterizes the nonempty parameter region supporting the first two strict political inequalities. The fixed-cost ordering guarantees a nondegenerate selection-free outsider-only interval.

\subsection{Continuation payoffs under full bypass}
\label{subsec:coalition-full-bypass-payoffs}

When the outsider alone adopts the bloc standard, a regional member obtains
\begin{equation}
    W_M^{SU,O}
    =
    K_I+2P+C
    =
    W^{IS}-(P-C),
    \label{eq:member-intermediate-welfare}
\end{equation}
so $P>C$ implies
\[
W_M^{SU,O}<W^{IS}.
\]
The outsider pays the fixed adoption cost itself and obtains
\begin{equation}
    W_O^{SU,O}
    =
    K_O+2P+D-F.
    \label{eq:outsider-welfare-bypass}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
    J=K_I+P-K_O-D.
    \label{eq:j-definition}
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{app:welfare} proves $J>0$ on the canonical domain. Therefore
\begin{equation}
    W^{IS}-W_O^{SU,O}
    =
    J+F>0.
    \label{eq:outsider-is-gap}
\end{equation}
All three countries strictly prefer IS to the continuation payoff under an SU once the full-bypass outsider-only state is selected.

\subsection{Strict-blocking benchmark}
\label{subsec:strict-blocking-application}

The canonical coalition concept is strict blocking: a deviating coalition blocks only if every deviator is strictly better off after the induced partition and the private-adoption game is re-solved.

When $F>2T_A$, no private adoption occurs under SU or SW. On $\Omega_0$, a regional member prefers SU to IS and to SW, while IS dominates the SU outsider payoff. Symmetry prevents one regional SU from strictly blocking another because the country common to both regional coalitions is indifferent between them. Thus all three regional SUs are stable.

When instead
\begin{equation}
    F_L<F<2T_A,
    \label{eq:intermediate-f-region-stability}
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:private-adoption} selects outsider-only adoption under every SU and no adoption under SW. Equations~\eqref{eq:member-intermediate-welfare} and \eqref{eq:outsider-is-gap} imply that the grand coalition strictly blocks every regional SU. IS also strictly dominates SW on the canonical domain, while any deviation from IS to an SU makes the deviators worse off after outsider bypass.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical strict-blocking application]
\label{thm:formal-coalition-stability}
For every $(c,v)\in\Omega_0$ under the full-bypass technology:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item if $F>2T_A$, the strict-blocking stable set is
    \[
    \mathcal S_S(F)
    =
    \{\rho_{12}^{SU},\rho_{13}^{SU},\rho_{23}^{SU}\};
    \]
    \item if $F_L<F<2T_A$, the strict-blocking stable set is
    \[
    \mathcal S_S(F)=\{\rho^{IS}\}.
    \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The theorem is an application of the continuation-payoff mechanism, not the paper's portability result. In particular, the high-$F$ part uses symmetry-induced indifference across alternative regional partners.

\subsection{Blocking-rule sensitivity}
\label{subsec:blocking-rule-sensitivity}

To distinguish the payoff mechanism from the coalition solution concept, consider weak/Pareto blocking: every deviator must be weakly better off and at least one must be strictly better off. Under exact symmetry and high $F$, this changes the result sharply. From $\rho_{12}^{SU}$, the deviation by $\{1,3\}$ to $\rho_{13}^{SU}$ gives country 1 exactly the same member payoff while moving country 3 from outsider to member:
\begin{equation}
    W_1^{13,N}-W_1^{12,N}=0,
    \qquad
    W_3^{13,N}-W_3^{12,N}
    =
    W_M^{SU,N}-W_O^{SU,N}>0.
    \label{eq:weak-blocking-indifference}
\end{equation}
The deviation fails under strict blocking because country 1 is indifferent, but succeeds under weak/Pareto blocking. Every symmetric SU is blocked in the same way, while SW and IS are also blocked by an SU. Hence the weak-blocking stable set is empty in the symmetric high-$F$ region.

The intermediate region is different. Every country strictly prefers IS to any SU continuation, and IS strictly dominates SW within the five-partition menu. The unique-IS result therefore survives the stronger blocking rule:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal S_S(F)
    =
    \mathcal S_W(F)
    =
    \{\rho^{IS}\}
    \qquad
    (F_L<F<2T_A).
    \label{eq:strict-weak-intermediate}
\end{equation}
The institutional robustness of the intermediate result comes from strict payoff gaps rather than symmetry-induced indifference.

\subsection{Small market-size asymmetry}
\label{subsec:coalition-asymmetry}

Now set
\begin{equation}
    m_1=m_2=1,
    \qquad
    m_3=1-\delta,
    \qquad
    0<\delta<1.
    \label{eq:secondary-market-sizes}
\end{equation}
No other primitive changes, and the private-adoption game is re-solved after every deviation.

In the high-$F$ no-adoption continuation,
\begin{equation}
    W_1^{12,N}-W_1^{13,N}
    =
    \delta(A-C)
    =
    \delta T_U>0.
    \label{eq:asymmetric-partner-gap}
\end{equation}
Thus the larger country strictly prefers the other large market as its partner. The symmetry knife edge in \eqref{eq:weak-blocking-indifference} disappears. For sufficiently small $\delta>0$, $\rho_{12}^{SU}$ is uniquely stable under both strict and weak/Pareto blocking. At the exact witness
\[
(c,v,F)=
\left(\frac1{10},\frac6{25},\frac15\right),
\]
this stronger result holds for every $0<\delta<1$.

In the intermediate region, the preference gaps have especially simple forms. If $i$ is an SU member and $o$ the outsider,
\begin{equation}
    W_i^{IS}-W_i^{SU,O}
    =
    m_o(P-C)>0,
    \label{eq:asym-member-is-gap}
\end{equation}
while the outsider satisfies
\begin{equation}
    W_o^{IS}-W_o^{SU,O}
    =
    m_oJ+F>0.
    \label{eq:asym-outsider-is-gap}
\end{equation}
Hence the unique-IS result survives sufficiently small asymmetry under both blocking concepts as long as the outsider-only adoption continuation remains selection free. At
\[
(c,v,F)=
\left(\frac1{10},\frac6{25},\frac3{25}\right),
\]
the result is certified for every $0<\delta\leq1/2$.

The coalition analysis therefore produces a hierarchy. The intermediate government preference and unique-IS result are comparatively robust within the canonical microfoundation. The exact symmetric high-$F$ stable-set multiplicity is not: it depends on the blocking rule and on an equality generated by symmetry. Section~\ref{sec:secondary} places this institutional dependence alongside the model's product-market portability limits.
