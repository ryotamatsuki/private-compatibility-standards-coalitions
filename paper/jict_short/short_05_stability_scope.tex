\section{Coalition stability and scope}\label{sec:short-stability}
The institutional application fixes complete cost relief ($d=0$); unlike
Proposition~\ref{prop:incomplete-adaptation-open-set}, it is not a
positive-residual-cost coalition-stability theorem. The formal menu comprises
$SW$, $SU_{12}$, $SU_{13}$, $SU_{23}$, and $IS$. Write a partition as a
collection of disjoint nonempty country blocs. A nonempty group of deviating
countries $\mathcal D\subseteq N$ forms exactly one new bloc, while the
nondeviators in each former bloc remain together. Consequently the induced
partition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:short-deviation-rule}
\rho'(\rho,\mathcal D)
=\{\mathcal D\}\cup
\bigl\{B\setminus\mathcal D:B\in\rho,\ B\setminus\mathcal D\ne\varnothing\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
Deviations that reproduce $\rho$ do not block. All firms re-solve their adoption
and product-market choices after $\rho'$ is determined; coalitions cannot make
transfers. A \emph{strict block} requires
$W_i(\rho')>W_i(\rho)$ for every $i\in\mathcal D$; a
\emph{weak/Pareto block} instead requires $W_i(\rho')\ge W_i(\rho)$ for
all deviators and strict inequality for at least one. The stable set
$\mathcal S_S(F)$ contains partitions with no strict block.

Let $\Omega_0$ be the nonempty part of the canonical domain where
$W_M^{SU,N}>W^{IS}$, $P>C$, and
$F_L<F^*=2T_A$ (with $F_L$ from~\eqref{eq:short-F-region}).
For $F>F^*$ no firm adapts, so, by symmetry,
\begin{align}\label{eq:short-institution-welfare}
W^{SW}&=K_W+H+2S,& W^{IS}&=K_I+3P,\nonumber\\
W_M^{SU,N}&=K_M+2A+C,&W_O^{SU,N}&=K_O+2B+D.
\end{align}
On $\Omega_0$, the exact sign certificates give
$W_M^{SU,N}>W^{IS}>W^{SW}$ and
$W^{IS}>W_O^{SU,N}$; see Online Supplement B.
For $F_L<F<F^*$, outsider-only adoption is uniquely selected under every
$SU$ while none occurs under $SW$. In this full-bypass continuation,
\begin{align}\label{eq:short-IS-dominates-SU}
W_M^{SU,O}&=K_I+2P+C=W^{IS}-(P-C),\nonumber\\
W_O^{SU,O}&=K_O+2P+D-F=W^{IS}-(J+F),
\end{align}
where $J=K_I+P-K_O-D>0$ on the canonical domain. Hence $IS$
strictly dominates the continuation of any $SU$ for all three countries;
it also strictly dominates $SW$. This proves the critical payoff bridge.

\begin{theorem}[Full-bypass stability application]\label{thm:short-stability}
On $\Omega_0$ and under strict blocking, the stable sets are
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F>F^*$: $\mathcal S_S(F)=\{SU_{12},SU_{13},SU_{23}\}$;
\item $F_L<F<F^*$: $\mathcal S_S(F)=\{IS\}$.
\end{enumerate}
The high-$F$ symmetric result needs strict blocking. The unique
intermediate-$F$ result survives weak blocking and small asymmetry.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof, including the exhaustive five-partition check]
Equation~\eqref{eq:short-deviation-rule} reduces all nontrivial changes to
splitting a regional bloc, switching one member to the outsider, or forming the
grand bloc. The following table shows, up to relabeling, a blocking deviation
or the reason none exists; the full finite coalition-by-coalition ledger is in
Online Supplement G.
\begin{center}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.18}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.18\linewidth}p{0.76\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Initial regime & Strict-deviation test and outcome \\
\midrule
$SW$, either $F$ & $\mathcal D=N$ yields $IS$; every country gains since $W^{IS}>W^{SW}$. Hence $SW$ is blocked. \\
$IS$, high $F$ & Any pair $\mathcal D=\{i,j\}$ forms $SU_{ij}$; both members gain because $W_M^{SU,N}>W^{IS}$. Hence $IS$ is blocked. \\
$SU_{ij}$, high $F$ & A member leaving alone obtains $SW$ and loses; all-country $IS$ loses for members. A partner switch to another $SU$ leaves the common member indifferent. No strict block exists. \\
$SU_{ij}$, intermediate $F$ & $\mathcal D=N$ forms $IS$; both members gain $P-C>0$ and the outsider gains $J+F>0$. Hence every $SU$ is blocked. \\
$IS$, intermediate $F$ & A one- or two-country deviation creates some $SU$; each deviator loses by~\eqref{eq:short-IS-dominates-SU}. The grand deviation changes nothing. Hence $IS$ is stable. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These tests exhaust the three regional partitions by permutation; the payoff
inequalities also show $IS$ strictly Pareto-dominates all four other partitions
at intermediate $F$ within this five-partition menu. At high $F$, changing
$SU_{12}$ to $SU_{13}$ via $\{1,3\}$ leaves country 1 indifferent and
strictly benefits country 3. It is thus a weak but not a strict block,
and the symmetric weak-blocking stable set is empty. Under
$m_1=m_2=1$, $m_3=1-\delta$, the indifference becomes
$W_1^{12,N}-W_1^{13,N}=\delta(A-C)>0$; sufficiently small
$\delta>0$ restores unique $SU_{12}$ stability under both rules in the
high-cost region. Intermediate-$F$ inequalities remain strict under the
certified small perturbations (Online Supplement G).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Limits of the political result} First, setting $v=0$ gives $W_M^{SU,N}-W^{IS}=c(13c-6)/32<0$ for $0<c<1/3$: a regionally favorable initial ranking is absent, although private one-way adoption can still occur. Second, allowing singleton compatibility groups to enjoy their own network effects also eliminates the initial regional advantage throughout the tested canonical parameter domain; this is a particular alternative network microfoundation, not a universal absence result. Third, in a pre-specified differentiated-demand setting with substitution $\gamma=1/2$, the initial regional member advantage and member-market exclusion component are negative throughout the audited parameter box under both differentiated Cournot and Bertrand competition, despite feasible outsider-only private adoption. These are substantive portability failures, not merely imperfect proofs. The differentiated-demand and singleton tests do not establish that every other specification fails; full calculations and parameter-domain certificates are provided in Online Supplement E.
