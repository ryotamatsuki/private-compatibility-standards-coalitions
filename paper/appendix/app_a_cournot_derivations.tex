\section{Cournot Derivations and Domain Checks}
\label{app:cournot}

This appendix records the technical regularity checks underlying Lemma~\ref{lem:cournot-blocks}. The closed-form Cournot solutions themselves are derived in Section~\ref{sec:product-market}; the purpose here is to verify uniqueness, positivity, and interior consumer participation on the canonical parameter domain without interrupting the main argument.

\subsection{Uniqueness of the four product-market configurations}

For a firm belonging to a compatibility group of size at least two, the own-output second derivative of market operating profit is $-2(1-v)$; for a singleton it is $-2$. Both are strictly negative because $0<v<1/4$. Thus the first-order conditions in Section~\ref{sec:product-market} characterize unique best responses.

Complete compatibility is a symmetric linear Cournot system and gives $q_I=1/[4(1-v)]$. In an SU member market, symmetry between the two bloc firms reduces the first-order conditions to
\begin{align}
1-3(1-v)x-y&=0,\\
1-c-2x-2y&=0,
\end{align}
whose coefficient determinant is $2(2-3v)>0$. The SU outsider-market system has the same coefficient matrix, with the marginal-cost terms assigned to the bloc firms instead, and hence the same positive determinant. Under separate national standards the reduced native--foreign system has determinant $4$. These systems therefore have unique solutions, which are exactly the quantities reported in Lemma~\ref{lem:cournot-blocks}.

\subsection{Positivity of equilibrium quantities}

The only quantity whose positivity uses the full upper bound on $c$ is the excluded outsider quantity in an SU member market. Its numerator is
\[
1-3c-3(1-c)v
=1-3v-3c(1-v),
\]
which is strictly positive when
\[
c<\frac{1-3v}{3(1-v)}.
\]
The same bound implies
\[
\frac{1-3v}{3(1-v)}<\frac13,
\]
so $c<1/3$. It follows immediately that $1-2c>0$, which establishes positivity of both $q_C$ and $q_S$. The numerators of $q_I$, $q_M$, $q_D$, and $q_H$ are strictly positive on the same domain. Hence every quantity used in the canonical continuation states is interior.

\subsection{Interior consumer participation}

The total quantities associated with the four configurations are
\begin{align}
X_I&=\frac{3}{4(1-v)},&
X_M&=\frac{3-c-3v+3cv}{2(2-3v)},\\
X_O&=\frac{3-2c-3v}{2(2-3v)},&
X_W&=\frac{3-2c}{4}.
\end{align}
For complete compatibility,
\[
4(1-v)-3=1-4v>0,
\]
so $X_I<1$. For an SU member market, the denominator minus the numerator of $X_M$ is
\[
2(2-3v)-(3-c-3v+3cv)
=(1-3v)(1+c)>0.
\]
For the SU outsider market,
\[
2(2-3v)-(3-2c-3v)
=1+2c-3v>0,
\]
and for separate standards,
\[
4-(3-2c)=1+2c>0.
\]
Thus $X_M,X_O,X_W<1$ as well. Since the equilibrium quantities are positive, each total quantity is also positive. The marginal participating consumer therefore lies strictly inside the support $[0,1]$ in every product-market configuration used in the paper.

These inequalities establish the regularity claims attached to the canonical domain in Section~\ref{subsec:model-domain}. They are also checked numerically over the domain by \texttt{code/verify\_numeric.py}; the numerical checks are regression tests and are not substitutes for the analytical inequalities above.
